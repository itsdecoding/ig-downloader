What actually happens to your file

Your browser decodes the image, draws it onto a canvas at the size you asked for, and re-encodes it. That re-encoding step is the compression: the picture is rebuilt from pixels and written back out with a smaller palette of detail. Nothing is sent anywhere, because the canvas lives in your tab.

Why the same photo can drop 80%

Phone cameras save at a quality setting far above what a screen can show. Dropping from 95% to 78% throws away detail your eye cannot resolve at normal viewing size, and the file gets dramatically smaller. Below about 60% you start to see blocking in gradients and skies, which is why the slider defaults to 80%.

JPEG, WebP or PNG

WebP is typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same visible quality and is supported everywhere that matters now. JPEG wins on compatibility — email clients, old software, print shops. PNG is lossless, so the quality slider does nothing for it: the only way to shrink a PNG is to reduce its dimensions or convert it to JPEG or WebP.

Transparency

JPEG has no alpha channel. If you convert a transparent PNG to JPEG, the transparent area becomes white. Keep PNG or use WebP when you need the background to stay see-through.

What gets removed

Re-encoding through a canvas drops EXIF metadata, including the GPS coordinates some cameras embed. The picture stays, the location does not. That is usually an improvement before posting a photo publicly.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?
No. Compression runs in your browser using the canvas API, so the file never leaves your device. There is no upload step, no server copy and no storage.
How much smaller will my image get?
A typical phone photo saved as JPEG at 75–80% quality comes out 60–85% smaller with no visible difference on a screen. PNG screenshots shrink far less unless you convert them to JPEG or WebP.
Which format should I choose?
WebP is smallest for photos and works in every current browser. JPEG is the safest choice for compatibility and email. PNG is only worth keeping for graphics with transparency, and it does not respond to the quality slider.
Can I compress an image to exactly 100 KB?
Yes. Enter a target size and the tool searches for the highest quality that still fits under it. If even the lowest quality is too large, lower the maximum width and it will fit.
Does compressing remove the EXIF data?
Yes. Re-encoding through a canvas drops EXIF, including GPS coordinates. That is usually what you want before posting a photo publicly.
Is there a file size or count limit?
No server limit, because nothing is uploaded. Very large images are bounded only by your device's memory. Twenty files at a time is a comfortable batch on a phone.
